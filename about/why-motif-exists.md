# Why Motif exists

I didn't set out to build a productivity suite. I set out to stop paying for one.

## Renting my own week

Quick capture lived in one app. My daily plan lived in another. The focus timer was a third, the weekly review a fourth,
and the automation rules that quietly filed things for me were a fifth. Each of them did its one job beautifully, and
each of them put the useful half behind a subscription — the reminders, the filters, the calendar view, the second
project, the reports I'd actually want after a month of use.

Five apps meant five logins, five bills and five copies of the same eight features. Worse than the money was the
switching: every time I moved between tools to do one thing, I spent the first minutes remembering where I was and the
last minutes deciding what to leave behind. The tools were supposed to protect my focus and they were spending it.

The day I noticed I was paying a monthly fee to *not* have a place to put a thought — that was the day I stopped
looking for a better subscription and started building a roof.

## One roof instead of five doors

Motif is the things I actually use, under one login that doesn't exist: capture in plain language, a daily plan that
checks its own capacity, a focus timer, an inbox for the things that don't have a home yet, projects and goals, a weekly
review, and rules that file and nudge for me.

Because it's one codebase, the pieces can talk to each other in ways subscription tools can't. My plan knows my
calendar. My focus sessions feed the same rhythm that decides whether today was a good day. My shutdown carries what I
didn't finish into tomorrow, and my streak forgives the weekend instead of punishing me for having a life.

And because it's *my* roof, a feature I want costs an evening rather than a plan upgrade. Nothing here is waiting on
someone else's roadmap.

## What the name means to me

**Motif** is a figure that repeats. That's a habit: the same small piece of work, coming back around, until it becomes
the shape of a week. It's also how the app was built — I took the pieces I loved from the tools I used every day, kept
the ones that fit how I think, and dropped the rest. The result is a blend, and the blend is the product.

Two warnings I gave myself while building it, because I'd rather write them down than pretend they don't apply:

- **It should not become a subscription I run.** No account, no analytics, no server. If I ever can't explain where a
  byte of my data went, the design has failed.
- **The streak should never become the point.** Badges are for the work — completions, focused minutes, days actually
  planned — not for opening the app. An app that rewards the login is just a slot machine with a to-do list on top.

## The trade I refused to make

|             | The subscriptions I was paying for           | Motif                                                                |
| :---------- | :------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Cost**    | Monthly, forever, per feature tier           | Free. The whole suite, no tiers.                                      |
| **Account** | Sign-up, verification, a profile to lose     | None. Open the URL and work.                                          |
| **Data**    | On someone else's server, in their export    | In my browser, in a file I can download and read                      |
| **Trust**   | Policy pages and promises                    | Every line is readable, and forkable                                  |
| **Fit**     | Whatever their roadmap says                  | Whatever my week needs — a feature is an evening, not a plan upgrade |

There's a real trade on the other side of that table, and I'd rather be honest about it than pretend there isn't: nobody
backs my data up but me, there's no support line, and features I'd get free with a big product are simply features I
haven't written yet. I chose owning the whole thing, including its flaws.
